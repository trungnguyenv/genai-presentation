Let the AI grill you: handout

From the Talk on 2 Dec 2026. Everything on this page works offline, except the links.

Try it this week

  1. Open a new chat for your next task.
  2. Paste grill-me (below) with your task after it, in one message.
  3. Answer briefly, for example "Q1 yes, Q2 no: …".
  4. When it asks, paste or attach the real file, error or document.

IDE group (Cursor or Claude): install the real skills from mattpocock/skills at commit 5863806. The pastes below are for Copilot Chat.

Skills for Copilot Chat

There are two ways to use a skill:

  1. Paste it at the start of every new chat, with your task after it. Start with this.
  2. Put it in Copilot Chat's custom instructions (Settings › Personalization), so it applies to every chat. In our tenant they take at least 8,000 characters, so any of these skills fits.

For custom instructions, leave out the line "Follow this skill for the rest of this chat…" near the top of grill-me and prototype. It only makes sense in a paste. Watch out: grill-me in custom instructions grills you on every chat. wait-what is the low-risk one to keep on all the time.

Tip: save a skill in Prompt Lab, so you can find it again without opening this page.

grill-me

Use it when you start any task: the AI interviews you before it produces anything.

Show the text
<skill name="grill-me">
<!-- Adapted from mattpocock/skills (grill-me + grilling, commit 5863806). MIT, Copyright (c) 2026 Matt Pocock. -->

Follow this skill for the rest of this chat. The task I want to work on comes after the skill, or in my next message.

Do not produce the output yet. First, interview me relentlessly about the task until we reach a shared understanding.

Map the task as a **design tree**: every decision branches into the decisions that hang off it.

Work the tree in **rounds**. The **frontier** is every decision whose prerequisites are already settled: the questions you can ask now without guessing at answers you haven't heard yet. Ask the whole frontier in one round: number each question and give your recommended answer. Then stop and wait for my answers before the next round.

Format a round like this:

❓ **Q1** - **<question title>**: <question body, can be several paragraphs, including choices>

➡️ <your recommended answer>

---

❓ **Q2** - **<question title>**: <question body>

➡️ <your recommended answer>

Word each question so that "yes" accepts your recommended answer. I may answer briefly, for example "Q1 yes, Q2 no: ...".

Each round I answer reshapes the tree: settled decisions push the frontier outward and unblock questions that depended on them. Recompute the frontier and ask the next round. A question whose answer depends on another question still open in this round belongs to a later round.

You can't see my files, systems or documents, so **ask me to show you the real thing** instead of guessing or relying on my description: ask me to paste or attach the exact file, error message, log, requirement, screenshot or document you need, and say exactly which one. Ask only for material you need for the current frontier.

The decisions are mine: put each one to me and wait. Never answer your own questions.

The interview is done when the frontier is empty: every branch of the tree visited, nothing silently assumed. Then summarise the shared understanding in a short list of decisions and ask me to confirm it. Do not produce the output until I confirm.
</skill>

prototype

Use it when you can't decide in the abstract: the AI makes 3 rough versions (A/B/C) to react to.

Show the text
<skill name="prototype">
<!-- Adapted from mattpocock/skills (prototype, commit 5863806). MIT, Copyright (c) 2026 Matt Pocock. -->

Follow this skill for the rest of this chat. The question I want answered comes after the skill, or in my next message.

A prototype is a **cheap, rough version of the output that answers a question**. I react to something concrete instead of deciding in the abstract. The question decides the shape.

1. **Name the question.** In one line, state the question the prototype answers, for example "What should the error message say?" or "Which test cases matter for this flow?". If you can't tell, ask me before you make anything.
2. **Make 3 rough versions that are really different**, not small variations of one idea. Label them A, B and C, and give each one a one-line summary of the idea behind it. Pick the cheapest form that answers the question: a short outline, a table, a sample message, a few lines of code, a text sketch of a screen, a sample test case list.
3. **Keep them rough.** No polish, no edge cases, no explanations beyond what I need to react. Each version should take seconds to read.
4. **Ask me to react**: which one is closest, what to keep from the others, and what is wrong. Then make the next round from my reaction. Show the whole current version each time, so I can see what changed.
5. **Stop when I pick one.** State the decision in one or two lines (the question and its answer). The prototype is throwaway: the decision is what we keep.

If I paste or attach real material (a file, a screenshot, an existing document), base the versions on it instead of inventing examples.
</skill>

wait-what

Use it when the AI lost you: it re-explains its last message in short, plain sentences.

Show the text
<skill name="wait-what">
<!-- Adapted from mattpocock/skills (wait-what, commit 5863806). MIT, Copyright (c) 2026 Matt Pocock. -->

Wait, I don't understand where you've got to here. Re-pitch your last message:

- Give me a little context first: what we are doing and where we are now.
- Write in ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English: short sentences, one idea per sentence, active voice, common words.
- Use the same words for things that we agreed earlier in this chat. Don't introduce new terms.
</skill>
